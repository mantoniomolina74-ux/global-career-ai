# Preamble

## The Purpose of This Constitution

This document establishes the foundational engineering principles that guide the design, development, maintenance, and evolution of Global Career AI.

Global Career AI is not defined only by its source code. It is defined by the decisions, principles, and architectural discipline that allow the system to grow without losing reliability, clarity, and maintainability.

The purpose of this constitution is to preserve the engineering identity of the platform.

It defines the principles that must remain constant regardless of future technologies, team changes, feature expansion, or architectural evolution.

---

## The Engineering Philosophy

Global Career AI follows a fundamental belief:

> A successful software system is not only one that works today. It is one that can continue working, evolving, and improving tomorrow.

Every architectural decision must consider:

- Current functionality.
- Future scalability.
- Security implications.
- Maintainability.
- Developer understanding.
- Long-term product evolution.

The project values intentional engineering over rapid accumulation of features.

---

## The Responsibility of Engineering

Every modification introduced into Global Career AI represents a responsibility.

A change must not only solve an immediate problem.

It must also:

- Preserve architectural integrity.
- Respect existing contracts.
- Avoid unnecessary complexity.
- Protect system reliability.
- Improve or maintain the quality of the platform.

Engineering decisions are considered successful when they create long-term value.

# Article III — Engineering Principles

## Overview

The Engineering Principles define the fundamental rules that guide the creation, modification, and evolution of Global Career AI.

These principles represent the technical foundation of the platform.

Every architectural decision, feature implementation, refactoring effort, and system expansion should be evaluated against these principles.

---

# Principle 1 — Clean Architecture

## Definition

Global Career AI follows Clean Architecture principles to maintain separation between business logic, infrastructure, and external dependencies.

The system must remain organized around business capabilities rather than technical details.

---

## Rule

Business logic must not depend directly on infrastructure concerns.

Infrastructure exists to support the business domain, not to define it.

---

## Objective

Maintain a system that is:

- Easier to understand.
- Easier to test.
- Easier to modify.
- Less vulnerable to technological changes.

---

# Principle 2 — Contract-Driven Development

## Definition

Contracts represent the formal agreements between system components.

In Global Career AI, contracts are considered the foundation of reliable communication between engines and services.

---

## Rule

Shared contracts must be treated as the single source of truth.

No component should create private interpretations of shared business behavior.

---

## Objective

Prevent:

- Interface drift.
- Hidden dependencies.
- Inconsistent data structures.
- Unexpected behavior between modules.

---

# Principle 3 — Strong Type Safety

## Definition

Type safety is considered a primary engineering control.

The compiler should prevent errors whenever possible before execution.

---

## Rule

The system must prefer explicit types over ambiguous structures.

Unknown behavior must be resolved through validation and clear contracts.

---

## Objective

Reduce:

- Runtime failures.
- Data inconsistencies.
- Unpredictable system behavior.

---

# Principle 4 — Security by Design

## Definition

Security is not an additional layer added after development.

Security must exist as a fundamental property of the architecture.

---

## Rule

Every component must consider:

- Input validation.
- Data protection.
- Error handling.
- Access boundaries.
- Information exposure.

---

## Objective

Create a system where security is integrated into normal engineering practices.

---

# Principle 5 — Layer Isolation

## Definition

Each architectural layer has a defined responsibility.

Communication between layers must occur through controlled interfaces.

---

## Rule

Components should interact only through approved boundaries.

Direct access between unrelated layers should be avoided.

---

## Objective

Preserve:

- Modularity.
- Testability.
- Predictability.
- Maintainability.

---

# Principle 6 — Defensive Programming

## Definition

The system must assume that unexpected conditions will occur.

Software must be designed to handle invalid states safely.

---

## Rule

Every external input must be considered untrusted until validated.

Every critical operation must define expected failure behavior.

---

## Objective

Prevent:

- Corrupted states.
- Silent failures.
- Unexpected execution paths.

---

# Principle 7 — Simplicity Over Complexity

## Definition

The simplest solution that satisfies the architectural requirements should be preferred.

---

## Rule

Complexity must always have a clear justification.

Additional abstraction should solve a real problem, not create unnecessary structure.

---

## Objective

Avoid:

- Over-engineering.
- Difficult maintenance.
- Unnecessary technical debt.

---

# Principle 8 — Maintainability First

## Definition

Code quality is measured not only by whether the system works, but by how easily it can evolve.

---

## Rule

Future developers must be able to understand why decisions were made.

---

## Objective

Create a platform that can grow without losing architectural clarity.

---

# Principle 9 — Observability

## Definition

A production system must provide visibility into its own behavior.

---

## Rule

Important system operations must be traceable and diagnosable.

---

## Objective

Enable:

- Faster debugging.
- Better monitoring.
- Reliable operation.
- Continuous improvement.

---

# Principle 10 — Documentation as Part of the Product

## Definition

Documentation is considered a permanent component of Global Career AI.

---

## Rule

Important architectural changes must include corresponding documentation updates.

---

## Objective

Preserve institutional knowledge and prevent loss of technical context.

---

# Engineering Principle Summary

Global Career AI follows this engineering philosophy:

> Build systems that are understandable, secure, adaptable, and reliable.

The objective is not only to create software that works.

The objective is to create software that can continue improving.

# Article IV — Architectural Laws

## Overview

Architectural Laws define the permanent rules that govern the internal structure and evolution of Global Career AI.

These laws exist to protect architectural integrity, prevent uncontrolled complexity, and ensure that future development remains aligned with the original engineering vision.

Breaking an architectural law requires explicit review, documentation, and justification.

---

# Law 1 — Engine Contracts Are the Single Source of Truth

## Definition

All communication between major system components must be based on explicit and shared contracts.

Contracts define the expected structure, behavior, and responsibilities between components.

---

## Rule

No engine, service, or module may create independent interpretations of shared business data.

The official contract definition must remain centralized.

---

## Purpose

This law prevents:

- Contract duplication.
- Interface drift.
- Hidden dependencies.
- Inconsistent system behavior.

---

# Law 2 — The Orchestrator Coordinates, Engines Execute

## Definition

The Career Orchestrator is the central coordinator of business workflows.

Individual engines are responsible for executing their specific domain responsibilities.

---

## Rule

Business engines must not directly coordinate other business engines.

Workflow decisions belong to the orchestrator layer.

---

## Purpose

This preserves:

- Clear execution flow.
- Traceability.
- Separation of responsibilities.
- Predictable behavior.

---

# Law 3 — Business Engines Must Remain Independent

## Definition

Each engine represents an independent business capability.

Examples include:

- ATS Engine.
- Scoring Engine.
- Ranking Engine.
- Decision Engine.
- Recommendation Engine.

---

## Rule

An engine must not contain responsibilities belonging to another domain.

---

## Purpose

Independent engines allow:

- Easier testing.
- Safer evolution.
- Better maintainability.
- Reduced coupling.

---

# Law 4 — Validation Is Mandatory at System Boundaries

## Definition

Every external interaction represents a potential trust boundary.

---

## Rule

All external data must be validated before entering the business execution pipeline.

---

## Purpose

This protects the system from:

- Invalid states.
- Unexpected data.
- Malformed requests.
- Runtime inconsistencies.

---

# Law 5 — Learning Systems Must Not Control Deterministic Logic

## Definition

Adaptive learning improves the system but does not replace deterministic business rules.

---

## Rule

The Learning System may provide insights, signals, and adjustments through controlled mechanisms.

It must not directly override core business decisions.

---

## Purpose

This ensures that:

- Learning remains safe.
- Business behavior remains predictable.
- AI improvements are controlled.

---

# Law 6 — Infrastructure Must Not Define the Domain

## Definition

External technologies exist to support the application.

They must not become the source of business rules.

---

## Rule

Business logic must remain independent from infrastructure implementations.

---

## Purpose

This allows future changes in:

- Databases.
- AI providers.
- External services.
- Deployment environments.

without damaging the core architecture.

---

# Law 7 — Every Layer Has a Defined Responsibility

## Definition

Each architectural layer must have a clear purpose.

---

## Rule

A component must operate only within its assigned responsibility.

Examples:

- API handles communication.
- Validation verifies input.
- Orchestrator coordinates workflows.
- Engines execute business logic.
- Persistence manages storage.

---

## Purpose

Clear responsibilities reduce complexity and improve maintainability.

---

# Law 8 — Architectural Changes Require Documentation

## Definition

Architecture is a living system that must preserve its history.

---

## Rule

Significant architectural changes require:

- Reason.
- Impact analysis.
- Decision record.
- Documentation update.

---

## Purpose

Future developers must understand not only what changed, but why it changed.

---

# Law 9 — Complexity Requires Justification

## Definition

Complexity introduces long-term maintenance costs.

---

## Rule

New abstractions, patterns, or dependencies must solve a real architectural problem.

---

## Purpose

The project prefers:

- Clarity over cleverness.
- Simplicity over unnecessary abstraction.
- Stability over uncontrolled expansion.

---

# Law 10 — Future Evolution Must Protect the Foundation

## Definition

Future versions must extend the architecture without destroying its principles.

---

## Rule

New capabilities must integrate with existing architectural boundaries.

---

## Purpose

Global Career AI must evolve while preserving:

- Reliability.
- Security.
- Maintainability.
- Understanding.

---

# Architectural Laws Summary

The architecture of Global Career AI follows this fundamental rule:

> The system may grow in capability, but it must never lose clarity.

The purpose of these laws is not to limit innovation.

Their purpose is to make sustainable innovation possible.
